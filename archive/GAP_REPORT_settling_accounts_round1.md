## Candidate codes (4)

### 1. `restaged-verdict` — status: **dominant** · confidence: high
**Register/tension.** Contested legacies get settled — or conspicuously dodged — by faithful restaging. The era's evidentiary image is the **side-by-side of copy and original**: Jaafar Jackson beside Michael, Dunham-then beside Dunham-now, the CG Animal Farm beside the 1950 Foreign Office propaganda strip, feminists re-photographing the Liberated Woman's Songbook shot for shot. Warm stage-glow recreation vs the cold adversarial documentary still. Tension: is beat-for-beat fidelity a verdict or a substitute for one ("official account" as pre-emptive acquittal)?
**Evidence.**
- Michael biopic (culturedmag, 008#94–009#101): Thriller/motown recreations, Jaafar in the red Beat It jacket; caption notes "the film positions itself as an official account" while Janet and Paris rejected it.
- Slate side-by-side composites (012#142 Dunham/Girls; 012#144 Jaafar/MJ) and avclub's Leaving Neverland counter-image (014#160: MJ and a boy in the car — the un-restageable photo).
- Mormon Animal Farm (artreview, 007#80–83): Serkis CG pigs against the 1950 UK Foreign Office cartoon commission — restaging with the politics swapped out.
- "Finding the Cattle Queen" (nplusonemag, 018#209–216): contemporary feminists recreating the Songbook images; steakhouse-pinup poster re-read ("Steakhouse royalty, feminist icon, fungible tourism graphic—she deserves a proper title").
- "History Has Its Eyes on You" (thedriftmag, 005#50): Hamilton torn-paper collage — the restaging of history as entertainment, literally shredded.
- Girlhood-retrial wing: polyester's tramp-stamp and Final Girl scrapbook collages (013#145–146, 014#163–164) — the 2000s' judgments on femininity re-tried in the glitter register of the girlhood it disparaged; afterschool's millennial-apologist TikTok (005#49).
**Quote.** "The movie is sheer propaganda, but for what?" — Travis Diehl, ArtReview (Mormon Animal Farm).
**Source spread:** 8 outlets, 4 categories (entertainment, art, intellectual, internet). Strongest code in the pool.

### 2. `canon-corrected` — status: **dominant** · confidence: high
**Register/tension.** Official commemoration corrected in place, and photographed doing it. Indoors: the white-cube retrospective where the previously marginal body of work is hung at full monumental scale, plus the late-career artist portrayed *in* the archive of their own life (Semmel in her studio chair at 93). Outdoors: the plinth as an account still open — the guerrilla feminist monument on the Columbus pedestal, Queen Victoria against demolition cranes, pink seesaws through the border wall. One gesture, two sites: the institution/public record admitting the original exclusion. Tension: correction administered by the same institutions that ran the exclusion — canonization as absorption. **Trajectory note:** the outdoor wing's toppled-statue vocabulary formed 2015–2020 and is now what clients default to (residual edge); the live edge has migrated indoors, and to *annotation* (LACMA declining to caption the Reyes controversy is itself the story).
**Evidence.**
- Joan Semmel at 93, Jewish Museum retrospective + studio visit (hyperallergic, 015#175–016#181): wall-scale feminist nudes from 1973–75 rehung.
- Nigerian Modernism at Tate (hyperallergic, 017#196–202): Onabolu, Enwonwu, Ojeikere, Egonu given the full-survey treatment.
- National Gallery Singapore (001#1–002#15): "Fear No Power: women imagining otherwise" key visual over a bronze figure group; SE Asian modernists as national canon.
- Lillian Bassman (thebaffler, 013#155–156): fashion photography "insinuating itself into the history of photography with a capital P."
- Lynette Yiadom-Boakye at Jack Shainman (artreview, 008#86): Black viewers before a gilt-framed landscape — the museum-gaze corrected inside the painting itself.
- Glorieta de las Mujeres que Luchan + Pedro Reyes at LACMA (hyperallergic, 017#203–204); Queen Victoria/cranes diptych (artreview, 008#85); Teeter-Totter Wall + JR's border child (thisiscolossal, 011#121, 010#120 — related-post images on the Robledo page; verify at round 2); protest photomontages (thedriftmag, 005#51–52).
**Quotes.** "I never started out to be an inspiration. I started out to just do what I've…" — Joan Semmel, hyperallergic. / "Feminist organizers later erected a guerrilla monument to victims of femicide on the plinth." — hyperallergic (Reyes/LACMA).
**Source spread:** 6 outlets, 4 categories (art, museum, intellectual, global). Meets the bar comfortably post-merge.

### 3. `stitched-testimony` — status: **emergent** · confidence: medium
**Register/tension.** The record of the wronged carried in deliberately humble handmade media — serape-stripe appliqué, linocut, ballpoint on prison paper — usually with the accusation or the love sewn in as legible text ("Papi no te vayas a olvidar de mi"). The material poverty IS the credential: pen and thread as what power cannot confiscate. Tension: defines itself against both the official archive (which excluded these lives) and the slick memorial commission.
**Evidence.**
- Arleene Correa Valencia, CÓDICE at Fridman (hyperallergic, 016#186–192): faceless serape-clad figures in papel-picado-style lace fields; work titled "My Dad Is Not A Criminal He Is A Dreamer."
- Pussy Riot's Resistance Imprisoned (hyperallergic, 018#205–208): ballpoint prison drawings, censor-stamped envelope, pink-balaclava placard protest.
- Eduardo Robledo linocuts (thisiscolossal, 010#110–117): Posada-lineage relief prints of Mexican heritage and community.
- "7 Artists on the Power and Urgency of Textiles" (thisiscolossal, 012#133–139).
**Quote.** "There is something simple about the pen on paper, which already tells a story about her condition" — Nadya Tolokonnikova, hyperallergic.
**GAP: only 2 outlets** (hyperallergic ×2, colossal ×2) despite 4 articles — fails the ≥4 independent-source bar as coded. Also fence carefully off Certified Human's countable-labor: here the payload is the *injustice recorded*, not made-by-humans.

### 4. `inheritance-reactivated` — status: **emergent** · confidence: medium
**Register/tension.** The inherited record treated as living material rather than sealed evidence. Two media, one gesture. **Photographic wing:** vernacular and family photography resurfaced in saturated warmth — pink backdrops, gold light, snapshot framing — restoring dignity to lives the record dismissed or never kept (Beirut sunbathers before the bombed hotel; four women on one motorbike against pink cloth), with the decaying-archive alarm (Nollywood tapes rotting) as its urgent edge. **Technique wing:** historical craft treated as source code and re-run through the contemporary atelier — reticella lace specimen boards pinned next to CAD screens and laser-cut lattices, family furs re-tailored, heritage architecture drawn as "[counter]stories" — with the lineage displayed *as part of the work* (research wall, archive board, process shot). Tension: warmth and mastery vs forensics — this code holds and continues the inheritance rather than prosecuting it; the Heritage-pole complement to `stitched-testimony`'s Judgment register. Watch the internal question the axis names: re-judged or just re-styled?
**Evidence.**
- Middle East Archive: Women النساء (polyesterzine, 011#124–130): Najla Said, Farah Al Qasimi, Hossein Fatemi, Yumna Al Arashi — "dedicated to amplifying narratives from the MENA region."
- Lucia Eames × nanimarquina (family.style, 015#169–174): archival drawings become rugs; the sepia family photograph (015#174).

- CCS BA "Cultural Histories" (1granary, 003#33–004#47): reticella/macramé archive boards (004#40–41) → laser-cutter screens (004#45–46) → shredded white runway looks; "grounded in personal reference points, like family, place, subculture, and mythology."
- ACM BA "Memory and Material" (1granary, 005#53–006#66): vintage fur on antique dress forms, denim distressing, heirloom-wardrobe styling (mirror selfie in the granny coat, 005#58).
- University of Bath / New Designers (dezeen ×2, 002#22–003#31, 006#68–007#77): "Kathalaya: A house of [counter]stories"; Golden Fleece textiles; Vietnamese-culture illustration; "secret history of plants" ceramics.
- "The Living Fragments of Al-Andalus" (newlinesmag, 008#92): gold-and-black illustration of the inherited form still inhabited.
